The Chapter-by-Chapter Workflow

This chapter is your day-to-day playbook. Once your room is set (Chapter 1) and Audacity is configured (Chapter 2), recording a whole book is just this loop, repeated once per chapter:

prep → record → self-edit → check against specs → master → export → log and rest

One chapter at a time, every time. The single biggest cause of abandoned audiobooks is trying to "record the whole book first, edit later". Don't. Per-chapter is faster, better, kinder to your voice, and recoverable.

The folder layout that never fails

Set this up once, before chapter 1:

my-audiobook/
├── manuscript/          # one PDF/text per chapter, recorder copy
│   ├── ch01.pdf
│   └── ...
├── projects/            # Audacity working projects (never uploaded)
│   ├── ch01.aup3
│   └── ...
├── masters/             # checked WAV masters (kept as backup)
│   └── ch01-master.wav
├── deliver/             # final MP3s ready to upload
│   └── 01_The_Beginning.mp3
├── pickup-notes/        # little text files of retakes to grab
│   └── ch01-notes.txt
└── LOG.md               # one line per session (see below)

Naming for delivery files: zero-padded, plain, sortable, e.g. 03_Cold_Evidence.mp3. No spaces in extensions, no emoji, no version soup. Opening and closing title files (if your store wants them) become 00_Opening.mp3 and 99_Closing.mp3.

Chapter 1 of a chapter: prep (10 minutes)

  1. Save the manuscript copy for this chapter into manuscript/ (recorder's copy: bigger font, wide margins, penciled notes allowed).
  2. Pre-read it once silently-aloud, marking emphasis, pauses, unknown pronunciations (Chapter 4 technique).
  3. Check the room: 30 seconds of silent listening at full volume (headphones) before you start. If anything hums, fix it now, not after two chapters of confusion.
  4. Water, apple, notes, log line open. Start the session log line: 2026-03-14 19:00 ch07 — session 1.

Record (45–90 minutes)

  1. Open the chapter's new Audacity project (projects/ch07.aup3, 44100 Hz project rate).
  2. Record ~5 seconds of room tone first: "session tone", silence, nothing. Leave it at the top of the project. (Chapter 3's spec needs this.)
  3. Read. On a flub: 1-second pause, back to the start of the phrase, re-read, continue. No "sorry". (Chapter 4 explains.)
  4. Between sections (scene break, chapter break, after any long pause for a stretch): record a fresh 2–3 seconds of room tone as a spacer. This doesn't cost anything and gives the editor clean splices.
  5. Ctrl+S after every take. Not "at the end of the session" — after every take.

Session rhythm that works well for most authors:

  • Read for 25–30 minutes. Stop, drink water, stand up.
  • Skim-listen to 30 seconds of each new section immediately while it's fresh. You aren't editing yet — just verifying the section is flub-clean and not too quiet on your mic.
  • Continue. 90 minutes total is a full session for most voices (Chapter 4: the fatigue rule).

Self-edit (30–60 minutes per finished hour)

In the same project, an hour of raw audio usually edits down in less time than it took to record once you get the hang of it:

  1. Find the mistakes visually first. Play at normal speed over each section you flagged as you recorded them. Flubs live between your re-read pauses — Audacity's waveform (zoomed in) shows the one-second silences clearly.
  2. Cutting a flub: select the flub up to the end of the cleanly re-read phrase; cut (Ctrl+X); done. Leave big natural pauses — they sound honest.
  3. Trim excess silence longer than ~1.5 seconds down to that (leaving breath is fine; a 9-second "staring at paragraph" pause is not).
  4. Mind the breath attack the wrong way: leave most breaths in! Only remove the harsh, loud, end-of-line ones, and cut around them without clipping the word that came before.
  5. Room tone maintenance: where you cut, keep the sound continuous. Paste-session room tone (from the top of the project) under any splice that sounds "unsupported". Subtle, cheap, invisible.
  6. Delete the session's first 5 seconds of "session tone" from the top of the chapter body — you'll place its own room tone in the export next.

Later chapters get faster. By chapter 4–5, editing is your evening podcast time with headphones and a slightly slower podcast.

Master, then check specs (15 minutes)

Run Chapter 3's recipe, in order, on the whole edited chapter:

  1. Amplify / peak to -3 dB max.
  2. Loudness normalization to ~-19 LUFS (subject to the plain-number spec sheet).
  3. Head/tail: ~3 seconds room tone at the top, ~1 second at the tail.
  4. Optional: check noise floor on a selected room-tone stretch (Plot Spectrum at around -60 dB or below).

If a check that involves "your room noise didn't get fixed" keeps failing, no amount of mastering will save it — improving the blanket booth by one more blanket will.

Export (2 minutes, per chapter)

  1. File → Export → Export as WAV → masters/ch07-master.wav (44100 Hz, 32-bit float if offered, mono).
  2. File → Export → Export as MP3 → deliver/07_Your_Chapter_Name.mp3 (constant bit rate, 192 kbps, 44100 Hz, mono).
  3. Ctrl+S the project once more, then close it.
  4. Update the session log line: 2026-03-14 20:15 ch07 — session 1: recorded 41 min, edited, mastered, exported. DONE ✓

That one log line is quietly why you'll finish this audiobook. Momentum you can see beats momentum you can't.

Pick-up sessions (20 minutes)

After exporting a chapter, you'll occasionally take a day's break, listen to the MP3 next morning, and hear one phrase that feels off (too rushed, odd emphasis, a syllable flub you missed).

  • Open the project, record the replacement phrase right into the timeline (using the same mic distance as that session; the room tone will carry the continuity), edit it in, re-run the master steps, re-export.
  • Same filename, overwrite. Done in 20 minutes.

This is normal, expected, and cheap. It's also the difference between "author-recorded" and "finished".

Momentum math (why chapter-by-chapter wins)

Per chapter of nonfiction (~5,000 words ≈ 35 minutes of finished audio):

Step Beginner After 5 chapters
Prep + pre-read 15 min 12 min
Record 90 min 55 min
Edit 50 min 25 min
Master + export 20 min 12 min
Total ~3 hours ~1h 45m

A whole novel-length book lands around 12–30 hours of work, spread over as many weeks as you like. A $200-per-finished-hour pro alternative for the same book costs $1,500–$3,000. Every hour of this workflow is worth about $100–$200 to you. It compounds with practice.
