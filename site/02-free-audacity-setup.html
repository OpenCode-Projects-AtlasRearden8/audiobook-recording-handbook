Free Audacity Setup: Five Minutes, No Money, No Tears

Audacity is free, runs on Windows/Mac/Linux, and has been quietly recording publishable audiobooks for over twenty years. There is no "pro tool" you need instead of it — it's just you, Audacity, and about five minutes of setup.

Getting Audacity

  1. Go to the official site: audacityteam.org (the .org — download only from there).
  2. Download the installer for your OS and run it.
  3. First launch: click through the welcome screen; check the Audio Host dropdown (keep it as MME on Windows unless something's odd; Core Audio on Mac; ALSA on Linux).

Look at the version number when you install. Audacity updates add little niceties now and then; any version from the last couple of years is fine for our purposes.

Setting your devices (one time)

On the toolbar you'll see two dropdowns next to a microphone icon and a speaker icon:

  • Input (microphone): pick your recording mic. Not "default", not your laptop's built-in mic — the name of your mic.
  • Playback (speakers): your headphones, so you can hear takes without loopback noise.

Then open Edit → Preferences → Audio (Windows/Linux) or Audacity → Settings → Audio (Mac):

  • Sample rate: leave at 44100 Hz for everything. (Chapter 3 will explain why this number is golden.)
  • Recording quality: 32-bit float if offered (it usually is) — it protects you from clipping quietly in the background.
  • Playback quality: defaults are fine.

Turning off the two settings that ruin audiobooks

Two Audacity defaults are actively harmful for this genre. Fix them once:

  1. Click "Overdub / Software playthrough" OFF if you see it in Transport settings. Hearing yourself in real time while recording a long narration makes most people stumble. Narration is you reading; monitoring the live feed is for music overdubs, not prose.
  2. Turn "Sound Activated Recording" OFF unless you have a specific reason. You want full manual control of starts and stops.

Your window layout

You'll see four main regions. Quick tour:

  • Top toolbar: transport buttons — record (red), play, stop, pause, skip to start/end. You'll live here.
  • Track view: where your recorded audio shows up as big blue squiggles (waveforms). Multiple takes stack as separate tracks.
  • Left of each track: name, volume, pan, Mute/Solo, and the Track dropdown menu.
  • Vertical sliders (top right): mic gain and speaker volume. Leave mic gain mostly alone and control loudness with your voice and mic distance instead (Chapter 1).

Recording your calibration test

Before your first real take, do this 2-minute ritual:

  1. Set your mic distance to about 2–4 inches (5–10 cm), slightly off-axis (see Chapter 1).
  2. Press Record and read anything out loud for 30 seconds — a paragraph from your book is ideal — starting after a two-second silent runway.
  3. Press Stop. Look at your waveform.
  4. Aim for peaks hanging around the middle of the track height, never touching the top or bottom lines. If you're in the middle now, good. If you're tiny, you can move closer to the mic or raise mic gain a little (you want headroom to stay under -3 dB peaks — Chapter 3 explains).
  5. Plug in headphones and play it back. You should hear your room as near-silence behind your voice.

If peaks clip (touch the 0 dB top), lower your voice level to the mic — pull back an inch or reduce mic gain — and retest. It's much easier to quietly amplify soft audio in editing than to fix crackly clipped audio at all.

Saving and file organization

Audacity's project format (.aup3) stores everything, including your edits. For the workflow in Chapter 5:

  • Save an Audacity project per chapter: one file in a chapter folder, named like ch07.aup3.
  • Record all your takes for that chapter in that one project, then edit and master there before exporting the final WAV.
  • Exported WAV/MP3 files are deliverables, not working files; give them their own subfolder.

The one Audacity habit to build today: press Ctrl+S after every take. Audacity has become stable over the years, but a power blink without a save has ruined enough narrator weekends that it deserves this bolded warning.

Keyboard shortcuts worth memorizing

Five keys save you hundreds of hand-moves per chapter:

  • Spacebar: play/stop
  • Shift+Space: play from last cursor position (listen-back)
  • Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y: undo/redo
  • Ctrl+S: save (see above)
  • X / hold X: (on a selection) Audacity's "zoom to selection" via View

That's genuinely all the interface you need. Everything fancier — noise-reduction, compression, EQ, mastering — is covered in Chapter 3 with exact plain-number recipes, and Chapter 5 puts it all in order.
